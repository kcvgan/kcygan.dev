I joined Ashby as a product engineer on November 3, 2025. Since then, I’ve completed onboarding, served as an onboarding buddy, conducted interviews, attended an R&D offsite, and shipped work I’m proud of. This collection of reflections captures my experience so far. I hope to write many more, as Ashby is the best place I’ve worked as an engineer.

From the start, I was greeted with kindness and trust. My onboarding began with small tasks to build momentum, then quickly progressed to medium-sized projects. At Ashby, engineers own their projects directly, with an almost 1:1 relationship between engineer and outcome. There are no traditional teams. Product managers and designers collaborate to shape direction, and product engineers execute. There’s no formal building process. Instead, we have tools, conventions, and intense calibration during the first three months. Feedback is abundant and aligns every engineer with the company’s way of working. Ashby’s [operating principles](https://www.ashbyhq.com/blog/culture/ashbys-operating-principles) have remained unchanged since inception, fitting well in the evolving SWE landscape. Intentional communication is also core to the culture. Ashby being email-first helps me stay focused and avoid constant distractions. Attention really is all you need these days.

From a technology standpoint, I would describe Ashby as efficiently pragmatic. We’re encouraged to push the envelope within the product, but to focus first on jobs to be done and customer impact, rather than academic engineering. I’ve always strived to work this way. My first exposure to product engineering was at Intercom (where I worked via Netguru), where I saw shipping without technical fanfare and with relentless customer obsession. That experience taught me to fall in love with the problem, not the solution. Ever since then, I have wanted to find a company that operates this way.

The engineers at Ashby are among the best I’ve worked with. Since most communication is via email or long-form specs, clear written thinking is essential. My biggest learning wasn’t technical, but about communicating concisely and doing the work so others don’t have to. Writing, revising, and rewriting specs to articulate decisions has been a constant exercise. Feedback has been positive, and looking back at my early documents, I see real progress in my confidence, decision-making, and output.

One core aspect of Ashby engineering is the concept of leverage. There are many things we do that multiply the entire team's throughput. This comes in the form of shipping reusable building blocks, improving performance, working on developer experience, or making the codebase more agent-friendly. Every engineer is encouraged to step outside of their own lane and look for opportunities to lift everyone around them up. When an entire organization lives and breathes this, it shows. There are so many things happening every day that you never know what amazing thing someone is working on or where your input and expertise might be useful.

As it stands today, I find joy in my day-to-day work. I am allowed to experiment with agentic coding, and as we all are, figure it out as I go along. The things I do allow me to punch above my weight and constantly improve, working with people I look up to and who never look down on me. My decisions matter, and I own the entire creative process from start to finish. I can choose how I go about my day and prioritize things accordingly. After an earnest day of work, I’m spent, in a good way. The creative itch has been scratched, and I don’t need a separate outlet as I did earlier in my career. I work more intensely than I’ve ever worked, which does not mean more hours. I believe we are indeed approaching a golden age of product engineering, and I’m glad I’ll witness and help create it at Ashby.
